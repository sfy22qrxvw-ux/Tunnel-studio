SOURCES READ
Four supplied PDFs represent three distinct papers. The two copies of the
Wang joint-extraction paper are byte-identical.

1. Ji et al. (2022), Precise Positioning Method of Moving Laser Point Cloud in
   Shield Tunnel Based on Bolt Hole Extraction. Remote Sensing 14, 4791.
   https://doi.org/10.3390/rs14194791
   Cylindrical projection, adaptive cloth simulation, DBSCAN, mean-shift centres
   and a project-specific bolt layout for ring/block positioning.
2. Wang et al. (2025), Extraction and location of subway shield tunnel segment
   joints from RMLS point clouds. International Journal of Digital Earth 18(1).
   https://doi.org/10.1080/17538947.2025.2528627
   Cross-section residual filtering, locally adaptive intensity valleys and
   normal/curvature support for straight and inclined between-segment joints.
3. Zhang et al. (2026), Automatic Detection Method for Shield Tunnel Segment
   Dislocation Based on Facility Point Cloud Removal and Segment Segmentation.
   Sensors 26, 4901. https://doi.org/10.3390/s26154901
   Offset-enriched trained PointNet++, local plane/normal bolt extraction,
   bolt-template seam positioning and common-reference dislocation measurement.

WHAT IS IMPLEMENTED, AND WHAT IS AN ADAPTATION
The papers mainly use ordered mobile scans. Tunnel Studio also handles static
and merged scans; it reconstructs a measured station/circumference map rather
than assuming scan-line order, scanner trajectory or a particular ring design.
No universally best detector exists independently of scan spacing, noise,
material, coverage and segment design. The following original combination has
explicit review and missing-evidence handling.

1. Robust lining reference and non-lining candidates
Use the existing RANSAC-seeded cylinder fit, local section centres and a measured
angular profile, followed by section-balanced longitudinal calibration. All
coordinates are local float64 during fitting. This accommodates measured floor
profiles better than rejecting every departure from an ideal circular cylinder;
it remains a one-branch, near-circular tunnel method, not an arbitrary topology
classifier. Check crown, walls and floor in the review.

Signed radial distance is positive outward, into a recess. Supported inward
points farther than the keep band (default 60 mm) are interior candidates.
Outward points beyond the recess allowance (default 250 mm) are a distinct
review group. Unsupported regions are retained. This is a geometric separation,
not semantic identification of equipment, rock, reflections or structural defects.
Only the Non-lining task automatically suggests excluding these two groups;
all detection tasks retain every group initially. Source changes require the
existing reviewed apply operation. Original coordinates and available attributes
are not rewritten by analysis.

2. Bounded full-density unwrapped maps
A bounded deterministic sample of actual points calibrates the lining. Every
selected retained original point then contributes to the map where lining
support and the keep/recess bands allow. Map cells carry occupancy, mean signed
depth and finite mean intensity. Missing cells are not treated as valleys.
Fast: 40 mm cells, up to 80,000 calibration points, 80 robust-fit trials.
Detailed: 20 mm cells, up to 160,000 calibration points, 180 trials.
More detailed: 10 mm cells, up to 280,000 calibration points, 320 trials.
The actual calibration sample may be smaller. Finer maps cannot recover detail
absent from the scan and are NOT accuracy specifications. Full-density labels
and exports are independent of the calibration cap.

Maps are capped at 2,000,000 cells. Oversized selections stop with an explicit
request for a shorter section or Fast; resolution is never silently coarsened.
Classification runs in 25,000-point chunks with cancellation checks. Spatial
sampling, model fitting and feature stages also check cancellation. Existing
background execution is retained; a performance guarantee for every machine or
scan size is not possible.

3. Bolt-hole candidates
Subtract an occupancy-normalised Gaussian local background (200 mm sigma) of
clipped reference residuals. Positive local depth must exceed both the user
minimum (12 mm default) and four robust noise scales (1.4826 MAD). A minimum
local occupancy prevents unsupported isolated cells from becoming features.
This is a depth-field adaptation; it is NOT the paper's CSF solver or its dual
local-plane/PCA-normal test.

DBSCAN uses occupied recess-cell centres with minPts=3. Euclidean epsilon is
1.8 times map spacing adjusted for observed occupancy, capped at four cells.
Periodic copies connect features across the unwrap cut. Sparse maps therefore
do not break long seams into spurious compact bolt candidates. Components must
fit the maximum diameter (350 mm default), aspect ratio <=3.5 and occupancy
compactness >=0.08. Gaussian mean-shift (up to 12 iterations) locates each centre.
These are compact recess candidates: drainage holes or other small recesses can
look similar. A hole connected to a seam or incompletely observed can be missed.
The exported centre is the projected mouth on the fitted lining. A separate
point_centroid is the mean of assigned original points; depth is recorded too.
No assumed bolt count, six-block template or project-specific bolt pair spacing.

4. Ring seams and longitudinal / between-segment seams
Seam evidence combines measured outward-depth valleys and, when usable,
locally dark intensity valleys. Intensity uses local normalised smoothing,
robust MAD contrast and measured neighbours on both sides in either map
coordinate. Missing/constant intensity falls back explicitly to geometry.
This implements the paper's complementary intensity/shape principle, not a
literal transcription of its formulas. It does not implement PCA normal-
curvature ratios. Low intensity alone is not a confirmed structural joint.

Ring seeds use angular coverage votes. RANSAC fits
  station(theta) = c + b*cos(theta) + d*sin(theta)
which represents a tilted ring plane in the local cylinder frame. At least 40%
of 36 angular sectors must support a fit. Current limits: seed separation 350 mm,
seed neighbourhood +/-350 mm and tilt amplitude <=450 mm. These are configurable
in code, not inferred universal construction dimensions. Extreme wedge rings,
very close seams and large bends may be missed. The fitting band is the larger
of 25 mm and 1.5 map cells. Ring names are ordered by local station.

Within each observed ring interval, periodic RANSAC fits arc = intercept + slope
* (station-midpoint). This permits sloping key-segment joints and separate,
staggered layouts in neighbouring rings. At least six of ten station bins and
eight measured cells must support each line. Absolute arc slope <=1; at most
24 lines per interval. Fit RMS and coverage describe fit support, NOT survey
accuracy. Fitted 3D guide curves can span unobserved gaps and are labelled as
candidates; the point groups contain measured original points only.

5. Segment candidates
Two ring boundaries and at least two non-crossing between-segment seams define
regions. Intersecting or implausibly close line fits do not produce panels.
No equal-angle fallback or assumed number of segments is used. Missing seams
can merge several physical segments into one region; every region is therefore
named a segment candidate. Areas outside complete intervals remain unassigned.
In All features, individually detected bolt and seam cells take precedence over
panel labels; the remaining panel points form each segment group. The Segments
task runs seam analysis directly, without a bolt-template assumption.

6. Review, location and export
Four named ribbon commands: Bolt holes, Non-lining, Seams, Segments. Setup also
provides All lining features. Each has Fast / Detailed / More detailed.
Colours distinguish retained groups; magenta is selection, red is exclusion.
Search named rows, isolate a group or right-click > Export highlighted. That
exports the group's original full-density points using the existing formats.
CSV exports feature metadata and world-coordinate guide vertices; JSON includes
features, settings, support statistics, local frame and review groups. Bolt
markers and seam guides also appear in Geometry & references, where the existing
properties and object export tools apply. They become unavailable when the
source scan mask/file changes, and are replaced by the next analysis result.
Export useful results before rerunning another task.

Station values are local fitted-axis coordinates, NOT design chainage. Ring
interval numbering is local to this selection, NOT installed ring numbers.
Angle is measured in the exported local u/v frame, NOT geographic bearing.
The original world coordinates are retained for downstream CAD/model use.

NOT IMPLEMENTED OR CLAIMED
No trained PointNet++ facility model: weights, training labels and a validated
static-scan domain adaptation were not supplied. No design-specific moving
bolt-template matcher without actual segment/bolt drawings. No CSF solver, no
paper's PCA-normal classifier, no automatic structural diagnosis, and no new
segment dislocation measurement. Existing analysis tools remain separate.
No reproduced field precision, no guaranteed complete extraction and no promise
of zero lag. Thresholds require tuning to the actual tunnel and scanner.

VALIDATION
See tests/validation_0_14_3.json and tests/lining_validation_0_14_3.json.
Known constructed scenes test bolt count/position, periodic wrap, tilted ring
planes, inclined longitudinal seams, segment regions, optional intensity,
plain-wall and occlusion negative controls, all three presets and full IDs.
API tests cover rotated large survey coordinates, existing exclusions, selected
areas, conservative defaults, CSV metadata/coordinates, stale guides, cancellation
and save rollback. Existing cleaning, review and installer rollback tests run
again. Chromium checks use production UI and WebGL with synthetic API data;
Windows/WebView2, the installed base application's reader binaries and Damian's
actual labelled scans have not been tested in this environment.
