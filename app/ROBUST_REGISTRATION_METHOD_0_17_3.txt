Tunnel Studio 0.17.3 - robust target-match review

PURPOSE AND LOCATION
Registration > Join scans > Review suspect matches checks the geometric
consistency of the target pairs already listed by the user. It is an optional
diagnostic before Solve geometry. It does not detect black-and-white or sphere targets,
fit its centre, infer target identity from its appearance, change cloud points,
or apply a scan transformation.

The current named-target workflow does not supply measured correspondence
quality. Small target sets therefore test every minimal combination within the
review budget; larger sets use reproducible uniform sampling. The engine also
supports progressive quality-ranked sampling when genuine match-quality data
are supplied, followed by uniform exploration. A target-pattern recognition
score is not a probability that two named targets are the same physical target.

PAPER AND IMPLEMENTATION RELATIONSHIP
Dhanya S. Pankaj and Rama Rao Nidamanuri, A Robust Estimation Technique for 3D
Point Cloud Registration, Image Analysis & Stereology, 2016, 35(1), 15-28,
DOI: 10.5566/ias.1378.
Publisher record: https://www.ias-iss.org/ojs/IAS/article/view/1378
Published PDF: https://www.ias-iss.org/ojs/IAS/article/download/1378/991/3515

The uploaded 14-page version was read together with the published version. The
implemented ideas are minimal rigid hypotheses, consensus over all eligible
correspondences, growing ranked sampling when justified, and local refinement
using an initially relaxed residual threshold that contracts to the base limit.
This is a bounded engineering adaptation, not a literal reproduction of every
printed equation, stopping rule, experiment, or feature-extraction stage.

The ranked-growth construction also draws on Chum and Matas, Matching with
PROSAC - Progressive Sample Consensus, CVPR 2005:
https://cmp.felk.cvut.cz/~matas/papers/chum-prosac-cvpr05.pdf

CORRECTIONS AND EXPLICIT ENGINEERING CHOICES
1. The paper's prose gives max(m, I/2) for a local sample while Algorithm 1
   prints min(I/2, m). The latter can leave fewer than the required points.
   This implementation uses max(m, ceil(I/2)), bounded by the available support.
2. A random hypothesis needs a complete all-inlier minimal sample. An individual
   inlier fraction is not that probability. The exact finite-set probability
   would be C(I,m)/C(N,m), and the usual inlier-fraction power is an approximation.
   This release does not present a probability of correctness or a statistical
   confidence derived from the paper's inconsistent stopping expressions.
3. The printed non-randomness/binomial expression is not normalized as written.
   Its extra exponent term and unspecified parameters are not used as a survey
   confidence test. Ambiguity and support are reported explicitly instead.
4. The paper leaves important threshold/annealing details unspecified and its
   pseudocode contains conflicting control flow. The explicit local thresholds
   here are 5, 4, 3, 2 and 1 times the user's base distance limit, with at most
   ten local repetitions when redundant support exists. Residual distances and
   the review limit use the same linear units; squared distances are used only
   for the fitting cost and RMS calculation.
5. The paper uses a 200,000-iteration experimental budget for cloud-feature
   correspondences. This target-table implementation defaults to 2,000 draws,
   accepts an engine budget up to 20,000 and at most 200 rows, and records its
   actual work. Exhaustive enumeration is preferred when C(N,m) fits the budget.
   Otherwise the ranked path reserves half the draw budget for uniform fallback.
   These are application choices, not numerical settings claimed by the paper.
6. Full 3D uses three non-collinear pairs, a proper SVD rotation and translation,
   with scale fixed to one. Keep vertical uses two horizontally separated pairs
   for heading and translation and requires an explicitly shared vertical.
   The latter is an application adaptation; it is not the paper's full 3D mode.
7. Coordinates are centred for fitting and residual calculation to reduce loss
   of precision at large survey offsets. Invalid coordinates, duplicate Fit
   positions and degenerate geometry are rejected. The input rows and original
   scan coordinates remain unchanged by the review.

The ordinary all-pair fit is a diagnostic comparison, not a prerequisite for
robust sampling. A gross distant mismatched pair can make that diagnostic fit
degenerate even when a well-spread correct subset remains usable. In that case
the baseline is reported as unavailable and robust subsets are still searched.
If no sufficiently supported valid geometry is found, no exclusions are proposed.

REVIEW DECISIONS AND PROTECTED OBSERVATIONS
Only Fit rows select the model. Check rows are independent validation and are
evaluated only after model selection; Exclude rows remain excluded. Kept or
protected coordinates cannot be dropped through the suggested-exclusion action.

The default review limit is 5 mm. It is a user-controlled screening threshold,
not a scanner specification, promised target accuracy, or acceptance standard.
Set it using the job's measurement quality and registration requirements.

A consensus must have at least m+1 agreeing Fit pairs and a strict majority of
Fit rows before exclusions can be proposed. Minimal support alone is not enough
to establish that another observation is wrong. Competing equally supported
transformations produce an ambiguous result. This protects against some repeat
geometry, but does not prove that the largest consistent group has correct
physical identities. Incorrect names can still form a consistent configuration.
In particular, three non-collinear corresponding sphere centres can determine
an ordinary full 3D rigid fit. They do not provide the redundancy for this robust
exclusion review: at least four agreeing Fit pairs and a strict majority are
required. Having a third setup does not add another common target to each pair.

The four engine statuses are:
  consistent   All eligible Fit pairs agree with the observed supported model.
  suspects     Some Fit pairs disagree; protected mismatches block adoption.
  ambiguous    Different transformations have equally strong observed support.
  insufficient There is not enough useful support for a proposed exclusion.

Residual tables distinguish Fit and Check results. Diagnostics record sampling,
seed, draw count, valid/degenerate/local models, consensus support and whether
every minimal combination was examined. A bounded, non-exhaustive search can
miss alternatives and is labelled as such; no confidence percentage is invented.
Reported search modes are exhaustive, uniform and ranked_with_uniform_fallback;
none indicates that a search was not run.

Review suspect matches does not change the target table or transform geometry.
It does not change point geometry or global target observations. Diagnostic
review/audit metadata is saved, and a stale geometry draft is cleared so an old
solution cannot be applied as though it represented the newly reviewed matches.
Only Exclude suggested matches adopts eligible proposed role changes. The user
must then run Solve geometry, inspect the new residuals, acknowledge their review
and use the existing Apply geometry action. A Stop review action is available.
The ordinary least-squares and registration-network solvers remain separate.

VALIDATION AND LIMITS
Use VALIDATION_0_17_3.json and its bound evidence for current numerical, API,
browser and installer checks. Earlier release evidence is retained as history.
Exact current counts are in the final validation index, not duplicated here.

The paper's experiments use six object models, point geometry and descriptors,
not RTC360 intensity target data, black-and-white target centres, labelled
tunnel target observations, or a 550-million-point survey. Its loop timing does
not include the whole feature extraction/matching workflow. The current review
operates on the small target table and does not scan the full cloud.

The user's latest sample is three merged setups with three common sphere
targets; the physical sphere diameter is still to be confirmed. The earlier
two-setup black-and-white target plan remains a separate historical context.
The new three-setup sphere job has not been validated in this release.
Native Windows/WebView2 and real-job millimetre target recognition accuracy
were not established on the Linux build host. Synthetic consistency, API and
browser checks cannot substitute for those independent field measurements.
This release reviews already identified centres. It adds neither black-and-white
recognition nor automatic sphere-target detection/centre fitting. CAD spheres,
inspection geometry and sphere coordinates in an imported format do not amount
to an automatic survey-sphere target detector.
