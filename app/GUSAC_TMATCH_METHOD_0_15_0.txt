WHAT IS NEW
Clean Tunnel defaults to GuSAC-based regional lining cleaning. The previous
measured-lining extraction, combined denoising, stray-point, reflection,
irregular-surface, lining-feature and SMRG tools remain available.
Tunnel classification runs GuSAC followed by connected lining clustering.
Individual clusters have stable categorical colours, selectable original
points, per-group keep/exclude decisions, hide/isolate/restore, and CSV/JSON
reports. The colour mode is independent of which points are kept.

GUARANTEED DATA BEHAVIOUR VS GEOMETRIC UNCERTAINTY
The code writes original int64 point IDs and int32 labels. It never snaps
coordinates to a circle, plane or fitted surface. Source XYZ stays float64;
RGB and intensity are not rewritten. Display sampling affects only the view.
Every selected retained source point receives one decision, including points
that are outside the learned lining or have insufficient evidence. Existing
selection volumes and prior exclusions still restrict the processing area.
No source deletion happens during analysis. The user reviews the result and
can create a separate cleaned scan or apply reversible exclusions.
This data-integrity contract does not mean every automatic decision is correct.

GUSAC METHOD
1. A deterministic spatial sample, capped at 400,000 actual measured points,
   learns one supported approximately circular tunnel branch. The sample uses
   a 15 mm spatial grid for balancing, but retains original point coordinates;
   voxel-centre coordinates are not used as observed lining points.
2. The existing robust tunnel reference supplies a direction and measured
   section-centre/profile corrections. It accommodates gentle curvature and
   supported floor profiles. Local station is projected distance in this
   estimated frame, not a design chainage or a rigorous arc-length alignment.
3. The default regional partition uses 5-degree angular sectors and 1 m
   longitudinal cells. Overlapping fitting support avoids input-batch seams.
   Sparse patches can extend their longitudinal fitting halo up to four
   configured windows. Original point ownership remains in the original cell.
4. Each patch fits an actual RANSAC plane. Candidate planes must agree with
   measured wall support, plausible normal orientation and spatial coverage.
   A dense foreground service cannot win solely by providing more points.
5. Deep narrow inward dents in the initial reference are checked against
   surrounding circumferential support. Only the reference is corrected.
   A patch still needs actual wall observations for its RANSAC plane; absent
   wall support is retained as uncertainty rather than invented geometry.
6. Local PCA estimates normals from bounded sample neighbourhoods, with a
   physical radius and degeneracy/support checks. Normal queries are batched.
7. All selected original points are compared with the regional plane and
   measured radial reference. Both must support an inward departure before
   that point becomes an obstruction candidate. Near-wall points also undergo
   the normal-agreement check. These decisions do not move source points.

CORRECTIONS TO THE PRINTED EQUATIONS
Equation 4 on page 7 is absolute point-to-plane distance:
    abs(a*x+b*y+c*z+d) / sqrt(a*a+b*b+c*c).
The printed Equation 5 multiplies normal lengths in both numerator and
denominator, which would be identically one. The implemented interpretation is:
    q = abs(dot(n, np)) / (norm(n)*norm(np)).
This is invariant to normal sign. Consistent surface normals have larger q.
Equation 6 prints a less-than cosine inequality while the accompanying text
defines a lower agreement threshold. The implementation retains adequate
agreement with q >= threshold. It applies the described mean-minus-1.5-SD
principle to supported inlier agreements, with an explicit angular bound and
small numerical allowance. See gusac.py for the exact safeguards.
The equation interpretation is documented; it is not presented as an exact
reproduction of inconsistent printed expressions.

DEFAULT CLEANING CLASSES
0 Supported lining: retained.
1 Inward obstruction/service candidates: proposed exclusion.
2 Outward points, joints and recesses: retained for review.
3 Insufficient lining evidence: retained for review.
4 Near-wall normal disagreement: retained for review.
The paper removes joint features to create separable blocks. Here those
measurements remain available, separately from the lining used for clustering.
This protects joint/deformation observations and sparse wall glimpses behind
services. Retained review groups can also contain real clutter and reflections;
their names describe geometric evidence, not verified object identity.

TMATCH INITIAL CLUSTERING
The paper names a neighbourhood distance R and minimum point count N without
fully specifying the preliminary clustering rule. This implementation uses
Euclidean radius-connected components of the GuSAC-supported lining. N is
the minimum final component size; it is not a DBSCAN core-neighbour count.
The default R is 15 mm and N is 30 points. R must exceed ordinary neighbour
spacing while remaining smaller than a resolved joint gap. A bridge of valid
lining points can merge panels; a missing-data gap can split a real panel.
The algorithm does not silently manufacture a seam from the expected ring width.

All original eligible lining points participate. The spatial grid is an exact
acceleration structure, not downsampling: its cell diameter is no larger than
R. Cross-cell merging requires an actual point pair within R, or a bounding-box
test proving all relevant pairs are within R. A 1 nm numerical allowance is
used at the radius boundary. Connections are merged globally
across input batches and spatial cells, including the circumferential seam.
Point records and component parents are disk-backed in temporary SQLite data.
Working trees/records and union-find traversal memory are bounded; no
full-cloud neighbour matrix is created. Up to 6,000 of the largest qualifying
components receive individually named metadata and review rows. This bounds
project and UI metadata; it is not a processing point cap. Eight representative
preview indices are recorded per named group. Every point in any
excess component remains in retained label 0, with explicit deferred component
and point counts and a warning. Label 0 is not claimed to be one geometric
component. Output ID and label files are staged and committed only on success.
Label updates use at most eight mapped windows of 8 MiB each, explicitly
unmapping evicted windows. Source selection uses bounded reads rather than a
whole-source mapping; neither mechanism promises a measured process-tree peak
for a billion-point run that has not been executed.
Label 0 denotes unclassified lining and is retained. Positive IDs identify
lining components and separate GuSAC review groups. The cleaning_class field
distinguishes these meanings; an ID itself is not a physical measurement.

RING GROUPING, GMM AND PLANES
Optional ring grouping uses physically defined local longitudinal component
centres, weighted by original point counts. It never treats arbitrary integer
cluster numbers as distances. Observed separated bands must have shared
longitudinal extent and substantial circumferential support. A positive expected
ring width is a consistency check; 0 requests observation-based grouping.
Weighted Gaussian-mixture EM refines supported bands. Ambiguous memberships,
inconsistent widths or missing joint evidence withhold ring labels. A seamless
shell is not split into fabricated rings. Local ring IDs are not design ring IDs.
Accepted rings also receive full-density centred covariance/plane metadata.
Every assigned original point contributes. Poorly conditioned normals are
withheld. Axial point spread is a ring-thickness statistic, not scan accuracy.
Unsupported planes have no axial_spread_m value; minimum-variance spread is
recorded separately without calling that direction the tunnel axis.

FIGURE 6 VISUALISATION
The classification view offers Individual clusters, Supported rings and
Cleaning decisions. Cluster/ring colours are categorical. Grey denotes
unresolved or retained boundary/review areas; red denotes excluded candidates;
magenta indicates the current selection. Selecting or hiding a colour does
not change kept points. Keep/Exclude changes the reviewed output explicitly.
The paper's Figure 6 is structural instance clustering, not semantic recognition
of cable, pipe, tray, bracket and other service types. No such semantic accuracy
is claimed here. Reports record cluster ID, name, cleaning class, ring ID,
original point count, decision and centroid.

DELIBERATE ADAPTATIONS AND UNIMPLEMENTED AUTHOR-SPECIFIC STEPS
The paper does not provide runnable source, full parameter sets or raw benchmark
clouds. Its minimum-spanning-tree axis operation is mentioned but not specified
reproducibly. Its GMM scalar field, component-count choice and geometric
reassignment thresholds are also incomplete. Tunnel Studio uses the explicit
supported reference, exact radius graph and physical GMM described above.
It does not claim exact reproduction of the crown/MST/piecewise-cubic axis
procedure or the incompletely specified adjacent-plane reassignment heuristic.
The six-block angular template illustrated in Figure 11 is not imposed on
other projects. Automatic named within-ring block types need the actual
project segment arrangement. Existing ring-plane cross-section tools remain
separate from the classification colour view.

VALIDATION AND LIMITS
See VALIDATION_0_15_0.json for the completed build's actual checks and measured
test results. Synthetic results and real-scan execution are recorded separately.
The article's F1 0.9527 and IoU 0.9055 refer to its ten-ring classification
evaluation, with manually prepared reference segmentation after GuSAC. They
are not Tunnel Studio results and do not establish an all-service removal rate.
The authors' acquisition density was at least 50,000 points per square metre;
the article acknowledges problems with data gaps and pipeline-concentrated
regions. Its normalized local CD/RMSE figures are not millimetre measurements.
Geometry alone may not separate a service touching the wall from genuine lining
detail. Severe curves, branches, irregular excavations, missing wall returns and
under-resolved joints need inspection or another workflow. Source preservation
and uncertainty labels make these cases reviewable; they do not solve them all.
Neither synthetic fixtures nor a smaller real scan validate the user's entire
600-million-point project or every Windows/GPU environment.
