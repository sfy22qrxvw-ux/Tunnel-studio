TARGET DETECTION — CURRENT FINDINGS (10 OCTOBER 2026)

The user reports many detections, none at their actual black-and-white targets.
This is an unresolved detection-quality issue. It is not fixed by Clean Tunnel.

Display detail changes only the points drawn in the viewport. The detector reads
the original cache independently, but the existing search is not an exhaustive
full-density classifier:
- Every input chunk is traversed.
- Candidate nomination samples at most 60,000 points per chunk.
- It retains up to 28 high-contrast cells per channel/chunk and 500 nominations.
- Gathering assigns each point to its nearest nominated seed, within 0.92 times
  target size; overlapping neighbourhoods can therefore split a printed target.
- It keeps at most the first 35,000 assigned points per nominated patch.
- The fitter caps its input at 30,000 points and trims to a dominant plane.
- A permissive partial checker score can accept three observed quadrants.
- At most 100 suggestions are returned. Suggestions are unreviewed, not IDs.

0.12 corrects misleading full-density messages and records sampling/patch limits.
It does NOT claim to fix detection precision or remove the algorithmic limits.

Next evidence needed: a close-up of the real target, printed width/diameter,
a small original scan crop containing that target and ordinary nearby wall,
with intensity and/or RGB preserved; ideally more than one range/incidence.
A screenshot helps establish the pattern; original points support validation.

Next detector work: full overlapping spatial neighbourhoods, spatially balanced
proposal fitting followed by full-point validation, stronger four-quadrant and
cross-edge evidence, a check of expected physical dimensions, tests against
stripes/shadows/joints/random texture, and separate partial-target/manual modes.
Tune against held-out real targets and negative patches; compare centre error,
precision/recall and support, not simply the number of detections.

The supplied Wang & Zha paper concerns geometric cloud registration keypoints,
not an algorithm for identifying printed black-and-white survey markers.
